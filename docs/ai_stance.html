Preface

I have noticed that I'm repeating myself again and again on the subject of AI; while being simultaeously tired of it. That page is some attempt at lowering that strain, by gathering everything in one place, and providing points to link to when necessary.

Nothing is set in stone. My opinions will inevitably evolve over time, and I want to avoid as much as possible to enter into dogmatism and polarization. It remains a complex topic which impacts the economy, job market, law, the planet and the environment, ethics, and much more. I will not be able to be exhaustive. I also want to try keeping it somewhat succint (try is the important word here, when the topic is so vast and heated). The intent is to make it evolve over time as my view evolves and/or when I learn new things around the subject. The last revision date is indicated at the top of this page.

If you want to reach to me about any of the topic I discuss below (or the lack thereof), feel free to do it as long as it is done constructively and with respect. If it is just witch hunting or finding a needle for public discourse, I don't see the point other than wasting each other's mental health and energy. The situation is painful enough as it is, I don't think being agressive at each other will solve anything or make anything better.

Final note: if you ask an AI to sum up all of this, well first it is super dystopic, and second it cannot be claimed as being my opinion or said by me, since the LLM's biases and hallucinations are de-facto part of the output.

AI Disclosure

If you are looking about AI usage in my projects, which is the case for code exclusively, it is detailed on the development page.

Definitions

This topics define words as how I use them, and is not intended to be authoritative or 100% accurate.

  • AI: When I talk about AI here, I mean generative AI, it is a shortcut that has been used pretty much everywhere now. But it is true that it also englobe machine-learning and deep-learning, and that gen AI is some form of deep-learning. This page focuses on generative AI exclusively.
  • gen AI: shorthand for generative AI.
  • AI slop: heavily or fully AI generated content, in the form of text (or code), image, video, audio, etc.
  • vibe coded: Form of AI slop where the user barely writes anything by hand, and use "agents" and "harnesses" with prompts to let everything be generated.
  • RSI: Repetitive Strain Injury. Englobe a bunch of things, such as arthritis, tendonitis, carpal tunnel syndrome, tennis elbow issues, clicking phalanx, etc. Desk jobs and jobs with repetitive movements (gardening, cleaning, cashier, etc.) are particularly exposed. (Being young doesn't make you immune to it between.)

FAQ / stance

Struturing the whole thing into something digestible is tricky. I'll try to go with a FAQ approach, with questions referring to each other. I know it is not perfect, but that what is fitting me best right now. Maybe one day I'll also do a diagram out of it, I don't know, we will see.

Pro or anti AI?

I don't like having to choose between black and white. But if I had to pick one, it would be anti AI. Nuances are covered in subsequent questions.

Why did you choose to use AI?

  • Strong pressure from the tech industry to "stay on the page", "stay in the loop", "not miss the train", not "become obsolete", etc. It was in some way to be able to talk about it during interviews, see what it could do, how it was evolving, etc.
  • I thought these usages (i.e. code auto-complete, code review, and in rare cases debugging and looking for new paths of information) were reasonable and not very harmful, though it was cautioning it in some way. As of how I see it now, see subsequent questions.

Did you stop using AI?

The only usage I kept is as a mean to find new paths of information, when I have exhausted looking online and asking around.

For gamedev projects, I fully stopped.

Why did you stop using AI?

For ethical reasons, loss of meaning, coping with RSI issues, and reducing noise. I always used free tiers besides 3 months of the basic GitHub copilot tier at the end, so the cost wasn't a subject for me.

Pros and cons of AI for you?

The cons far outweight the pros. And there are more cons than pros. That being said:

Pros:

  • For code reviews, it can help catch dumb mistakes, and sometimes bugs or vulnerabilities. But everything has to be taken with a grain of salt, and it can be spammy.
  • For code completion of recurrent and predictable code (end of line of a for loop, very clear pattern that can't be factored, translation of a type to another programming language which I had to do once), it can be useful. The gain of time is debatable, but the strain on hands is reduced. Which is inevitably a pro when you faced RSI issues.
  • I think it can be helpful for semantic search while not replacing classical search algorithms and techniques.

Cons:

Deep breath:

  • Destruction of jobs, at least in creative fields.
    • There is debate regarding dev jobs. What I can say is that in France, IT jobs declined more sharply than other jobs. It is being used as an excuse, but I also think it has an impact. At least for junior positions. I have read testimonials of people saying their companies freeze junior employment because a senior with AI could cover the job of multiple junior devs. Is it true, I don't know, but some employers do, so it does have an impact regardless.
  • Big environmental cost:
    • Land artificialisation (through giant datacenters replacing agricultural and protected land.)
      • Google deforested a protected forest for a datacenter project in Finland.
      • Massive projects in Texas, U.S.
    • Local energy stress.
      • Some projects require the power of a nuclear plant to function.
      • It may drive up the electricity of individuals, when it is already going up with the many different local and global crises people face.
      • Training is especially costly.
    • Local water stress.
      • Yes, some projects use a closed circuit. Certainly not all of them.
      • Texas, U.S. see many projects being established on top of a fossil phreatic table that already went from 80m to 35m deep (roughly).
      • Electronic components production uses a lot of water.
    • Rise of greenhouse gas emissions.
      • Big tech footprint has accelerated with AI development.
      • We are far from a world exclusively powered by renewable, carbon free energy production.
      • It is not just about energy usage, but also about components production.
    • Mass e-waste production.
      • Like cryptocurrencies, it pushes components to the limits then trash them, at an industrial scale. Smartphones, computers, and IoT already create a massive problem by themselves, this probably doubles it for questionable benefits. We are not talking about curing cancer, but allowing John Foobarbaz to have a Ghibli stylized avatar as a profile picture.
  • Wrecking up societies and economies:
    • Highly disturbing the consummer market.
      • Graphic cards, RAM, SSDs, are exploding in price. While a bunch of them are just dorment in warehouses because of economical speculation and manipulation.
    • It creates yet another dependency on big tech.
      • They are not known for being ethical nor pro-democracy.
      • Lack of tech sovereignty is a ticking bomb and a weapon for those hosting the infrastructure.
  • It undermine democracies and spread misinformation.
    • Everyone can easily create deepfakes of politicians, activists, etc.
    • Search results have more AI slop "articles" than human-authored content (not even talking about verified content.)
    • People blindly trusting a black box throwing a bunch of text without double fact-checking is a propaganda tool that dictators probably dreamt of for decades (and it is probably why they invest money into AI.)
    • It reinforces stereotypes of racism, antisemitism, sexism, lgbt-phobia, discrimination against minorities and disabled people, and so on.
  • It violates copyright and intellectual property.
    • Obvious examples:
      • Scraping the whole web without consent nor checking licenses.
      • Microsoft using GitHub public (and probably private too) repositories for training Open AI GPT models.
      • Anthropic scanning and destroying a bunch of physical books, including rare and limited editions. (No, destroying it doesn't mean you paid your due, Buying a CD album doesn't mean I can then use all its content in a film production. And it is ridiculously small compared to the cost of all the jobs it destroys on the other side. So stealing people's work to then push them to living in the streets.)
      • Suno scrapping YouTube, Spotify, and other platforms to train their models regardless of copyright.

    • Nuancing: saying a few lines of LLM code means copyright violation is not true to me. Because nobody can copyright a few lines of code, it doesn't make sense. The very exact syntax in the very exact programming language of it eventually, but that's it. The only case when it is possible is software patents. It only exists in some countries, and it just do a bunch of harm, reinforces monopolies and abusive commercial practices, and prevents any individual or small actor, commercial or non-commercial, to have a chance to enter the industry in some cases. One obvious example being the H264/MPEG/MP3/MP4 patents story. (In my opinion software patents do not make sense).

      I think it becomes a violation when there was either an obvious intent or a strong proof or it, intentional or not. For example:

      • Using a prompt to reverse engineer an exe and produce a competitor software.
      • Taking some files or an entire FOSS project as-is to modify it and ship it without respecting the original license.
        • In that case, I consider AI companies to have violated copyright, while it's a case by case situation for its users that can't be summed up in a few lines.
  • This list is not exhaustive, I probably forgot stuff, It'll be amended with time.

Opinion on full AI bans?

I think it is a harmful approach, somewhat dogmatic, that also excludes any form of nuance. Having all the full anti AI on one side and all the turbo AI bros on the others means anybody with nuance (being in the usage itself, or stopping it, or anything else that fits that word) is de facto excluded and ostracized. (And creating a third group for them is even worse.) I see it as a symptom of the trend of polarization and disparition of nuances, which can't do good in the long term. And also as a symptom of the increasing online social pressure where you are not allowed to make any mistake. (I don't talk about killing or sexual assaults, and even still, jumping into rumors or accusations without fact-checking is weaponized even if by a minority. And yes judicial bodies are deffective on the subject of sexual assaults, fact checking can also happen with independant press and mass testimonials. It's a entire subject by itself so I'll stop here. But it again comes down to: it's not black or white).

Am I biased because I face that personally (getting away from AI use in code but with many projects left "tainted")? Maybe. But I don't think it invalidates my point.

Ultimately I see it as witch hunting, seeing people that used it even partially as permanently damned and not worth consideration. Now, I know there is also the fact that people are tired of seeing AI everywhere (myself included), that it is a nightmare to moderate, that some industries like gamedev and other artistic mediums are in a survival mode and defending themselves rather than willfully attacking others, etc. I recognize it's a complex topic. But I want to mention that it does hurt, intentionally or not, and that it should be kept in mind (and eventually worded in policies/codes of conduct) when taking such drastic decisions.

Opinion on AI disclosures?

I think it is a good thing. It is about transparency for consumers, the same way we have it, to some extent (and depending of countries/regions) for food, for instance. However I think it critically lacks nuances right now. And just providing a block of text for that is duct tape, not a solution.

For instance, if we take a video-game, I'd like to be able to know:

  • For what it was used: graphics, code, music, translation, voice-overs, etc.
  • A gradient for each of them: Rough estimation of how much, with some criterias to avoid having it being too subjective.
    • A mere checkbox puts a fully vibe-coded low effort product in the same basket as, say, Big Walk where the vast majority of the work is human-authored, without entering into the details of that controversy, which is also ultimately about lack of clear communication and consummer information.
  • Details per topic.
    • For assets: for placeholders? production content? concept art? For modifications or full generations? What coverage, which assets? etc.
    • For code: for text-auto complete (a few lines or entire functions/files) ? Using agents? For code review? For debugging? With AI output in the final code? Is it about the source code itself or about first-party/third-party dependencies? etc.

I also think it is important to make it mandatory, the same way we do for food ingredients. Then it is up to the consumer to pick what it wants based on their ethics and political values (like it is already the case for vegan food for example).

That doesn't mean full AI free spaces shouldn't be able to exist. This is mostly relevant for the most prominent spaces like stores with a large public such as Steam, itch.io, the Play Store and App Store, console stores, etc.

Will you stop working on "AI tainted" projects?

No, and for multiple reasons:

  • I care too much about these projects to stop working on them, even if just for personal use/enjoyment. And I still care about and like sharing them. I assume people can filter things by themselves. (And I personally also prefer filters over bans, when we are not talking about things like racism, forms of discrimination, and so on, I assume you see my point here.)
  • I like to be able to refer to my previous work when seeking solution to problems. For some, it means the project becomes de facto AI tainted, even if there is no code transfer or because it is in my mind. And, like, there is nothing I can do about that. (Between it is something I weigh in to some extent when doing the discosures, even if I think it is a bit of a stretch.)
    • And if you think that way and try to find the needle to put my work down or suspect anything I made, I don't know, just move on? It will be better for both your mental health and mine. I don't want to spend my entire life justifying every single action I do for the look of others. And I don't think it's healthy either.
  • Nothing guarantees it will solve the issue, people will always find something to complain about and to invalidate everything. Reactions to the Big Walk controversy shows it to an extent even if it comes out of lack of transparency from House House.
